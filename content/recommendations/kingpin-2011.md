There's a line in society. On one side of it you're a "good guy" and on the other side you aren't anymore. At least that's what we're told growing up. Kevin Poulsen, is a complex figure who bobs and weaves back and forth across the line. He genuinely seems to not care about the line, and sometimes to not be able to see it all. For him, the important thing is doing what he loves, hacking. The art of making things do more than they were designed. It is a sort of malicious compliance of technology.

Ultimately, Poulsen ends up as a journalist and works for US law enforcement, mainly the FBI. In that capacity he helps uncover a clandestine world of crime where the cyber and the physical mingle. A world where blood, cash, and bits are all currency to be exchanged.

## Recommendation

Books like this one are very important for anyone who wants to be a practitioner. They go beyond any arbitrary confines of a textbook and dive deep into the real world. Told from the perspective of a hacker, it allows the audience a point of view that is foreign to most. Coupled with [Ghost in the Wires](/recommendations/ghost-in-the-wires-2011) by [Kevin Mitnick](/creators/kevin-mitnick), these books should be considered essential reading.
