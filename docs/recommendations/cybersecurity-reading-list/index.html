Cybersecurity Reading List

created: 2026-08-01

I hope to create dedicated pages for the most notable of entries on this list, but for now just need a place to quickly jot them all down.

Books

While I tend to prefer nonfiction titles for this genre, there are some notable works of fiction that I feel are good reads for interested parties to be familiar with as well.

Nonfiction

  • Hackers: Heroes of the Computer Revolution by Steven Levy, 1984. A series of stories about the people who created modern computing.
  • The Cuckoo’s Egg by Clifford Stoll, 1989. A junior university employee’s fall down the rabbit hole of international hacking, law enforcement, national security, and cyberspace in the twilight years of the Cold War.
  • Cyberpunk: Outlaws and Hackers on the Computer Frontier by Katie Hafner and John Markoff, 1992. From the original Dark Side Hacker, Kevin Mitnick, to the Hanover Hackers that Clifford Stoll battles in The Cuckoo’s Egg, this book covers some of the most notable hackers of the time.
  • Crypto: How the Code Rebels Beat the Government - Saving privacy in the Digital Age by Steven Levy, 2001. Very much a follow up to his earlier work, this time zeroing in on cryptography and privacy rights.

Fiction

  • Cryptonomicon by Neal Stephenson, 1999. A head of its time, this book envisions what we would now call Stable Coin, and looks at the practical application of cryptography following two intertwined stories spanning WW2 and the 1990s. One of my all time favorite books, and a must read.

Movies

  • Hackers staring Jonny Lee Miller, Angelina Jolie, and Fisher Stevens, 1995. A fictional tale of a young man transplanted from Seattle to New York who finds friends in the local hacker scene, while trying to escape his past. While the story is fictional, everything else is based of the lives, actions, and prosecutions of New York City hackers in 1990s.

Online Media

Podcast, YouTube channels, blogs, and dating profiles. Anything that doesn’t fit in the above categories that you primarily access online will end up here.

Podcasts

  • Black Hills Information Security Information security professionals discussing the week’s news stories.

YouTube Channels

  • Fireship Code tutorials and tech news with ample snark and 2020s era editing.

  • Linus Tech Tips A grab bag of content that spans topics and the last 20+ years. While perhaps not the most directly useful for seasoned pros, the channel offers a lot of quick explainer videos that are well produced and easy to digest when sent to less tech savvy friends and family.

  • Low Level Breakdowns of all the latest vulnerabilities from a researcher with 10+ years experience.

  • You Suck at Programming Primarily a bash coding channel, but shows some very low-level *nix workings.

Websites/Blogs

  • Dark Reading 20 years of reporting on cybersecurity.

  • KrebsOnSecurity A blog from one of the top journalists in the field. Find out about his surprising origin story in Spam Nation.
